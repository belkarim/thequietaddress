Athens

Whitewash, marble and a light that hides nothing.

We keep one apartment in Athens, on a roof in Psyri with the Acropolis in front of it. We are looking, slowly, for a second.

Athens is not a pretty city, and that is the point. It is a working capital of concrete and marble, stairwells and balconies, where the ancient sits in the middle of the everyday and nobody makes a fuss. The light does the rest: it is direct, white, and generous until late.

The person who holds the keys has welcomed guests here for more than a decade, and knows which taverna will still feed you at midnight and which museum is empty on a Tuesday.

Athens, our way

A guide to the city, written by the person who holds the keys.
Mornings

The rock at eight, the museum at ten

Be at the Acropolis gate when it opens and walk down the south slope afterwards. The Acropolis Museum is best before the coaches arrive and the marble floors are still cool.

Afternoons

Shade, and the neighbourhoods

Anafiotika's whitewashed lanes after four, when the groups have gone. Then Koukaki or Pangrati for a coffee under a plane tree, where the city lives rather than performs.

Evenings

Tables without menus in English

Ask us. We keep a list of tavernas where the food arrives before you ask, and where the bill is a fraction of what you feared.
