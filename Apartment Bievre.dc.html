Rue de Bièvre

Two windows on the tower and spire of Notre-Dame, in one of the oldest streets in Paris.

Sleeps 2 · 31 m² · Fourth floor, no lift · Four to fourteen nights

Rue de Bièvre is a cobbled lane that has run from the Seine into the Latin Quarter since the thirteenth century. The house is from the sixteenth or seventeenth; the staircase is listed, which is why there is no lift, and why the four flights up are part of the story rather than a footnote.

At the top, an architect has made one bright room out of thirty-one square metres: white walls, pale timber, a sofa that becomes a proper bed at night, a dining table, and a kitchen with an induction hob that takes up less space than it should. The shower room is walk-in and new.

Then the two tall windows, which are the reason for the apartment. They face the cathedral: the south tower, the roof, and the spire that went up again in 2024, framed by the chimney pots of the fifth arrondissement. The bells carry across the water. In the evening the stone turns the colour of honey; in the morning the light comes off it into the room. You will find yourself eating at the window rather than the table.

For one person or a couple who want the oldest Paris, the best view in it, and the newest plumbing.

The neighbourhood

A quiet lane between Notre-Dame and the Latin Quarter.

The street has housed scholars, painters and a president of the Republic, and it stays residential and quiet. The quai is at one end, the place Maubert market at the other. The cathedral you see from the window is two minutes away, across the pont de l'Archevêché; Saint-Germain-des-Prés fifteen minutes along the Seine.

Neighbourhood guide

The Left Bank, under the towers.

Start with the cathedral, since you have been looking at it over breakfast; go before nine, when the queue is short and the nave is still cold. Then down to the quai de la Tournelle and left, with the Île Saint-Louis across the water. The bouquinistes open late morning, and the best of them are on this bank.

Place Maubert has a market on Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday mornings; buy cheese and fruit there and eat on the quai. Rue Mouffetard is fifteen minutes uphill and worth it once. Shakespeare and Company is five minutes away and worth it every day it rains.

For dinner, ask us. The Latin Quarter has a hundred tourist restaurants and a dozen good ones, and we know which is which.
