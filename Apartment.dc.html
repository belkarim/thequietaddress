Saint-Merri

A beamed studio on rue Saint-Martin, facing the church of Saint-Merri.

Sleeps 2 · Studio · Fourth floor, no lift · Four to fourteen nights

The house dates from the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries and is listed, which explains the beams across the ceiling, the narrow, uneven stair, and the absence of a lift. Take the four flights slowly; the studio at the top is worth it.

Two windows face the flamboyant Gothic front of Saint-Merri across the pedestrian street, so the room is bright all day and nobody looks in. Under the beams: a sofa that opens into a 140 cm bed, an open wardrobe, two low tables, a television you may ignore. The kitchen is small and complete, down to the washing machine; the shower room is compact and well kept.

You could not be more central. Hôtel de Ville, the Marais, the Pompidou and the quais are all a few minutes on foot, and the street itself is pedestrian. Being in the very middle of Paris, it is not always silent at night; we say so because we would want to know.

The neighbourhood

Between the Pompidou and the Seine, in a street cars gave up on.

Rue Saint-Martin is one of the two Roman roads of Paris, and this stretch of it has been pedestrian for decades. The Centre Pompidou is two minutes north, the Hôtel de Ville three minutes south, the Seine and the Île de la Cité five. Châtelet, the largest métro hub in the city, is a four-minute walk.

Neighbourhood guide

Beaubourg, in five walks.

Turn right out of the door and the Pompidou is in front of you in two minutes; the escalator to the top is worth the ticket for the view alone, and the library is free. Turn left and rue Saint-Martin runs down past the Tour Saint-Jacques to the river and the Île de la Cité.

For coffee, the lanes behind Saint-Merri towards rue Quincampoix are quieter than the square in front. Cross rue de Rivoli and you are in the Marais in five minutes; rue des Rosiers on a Sunday morning is closed to cars and everyone you know is there, so go early or go late.

If it rains, and it will, the Pompidou and the Musée des Arts et Métiers are both ten minutes away and both dry. If the sun comes out, the tip of the Île Saint-Louis at six in the evening is the best seat in the city and nobody charges for it.
